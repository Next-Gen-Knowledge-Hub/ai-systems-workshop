# Exploring the cognitive agent that thinks, monitors, and adapts

Stacking chain-of-thought, ReAct, trees, and reflection gives you a toolbox. Cognition is the craftsperson who picks a tool, watches whether it is working, and switches when it is not. Treat that as engineering you can measure.

Five production failures show up as soon as a capable agent meets a messy question. It answers confidently from weak evidence. It repeats a dead search. It keeps a plan after the facts have changed. It guesses past what it knows. It cannot join two lookups into one comparison. Each gap needs a module, and a stronger prompt will not grow that module.

- **Cognition** is the quality of the agent's model of the task: split a new problem into parts, respect which parts depend on which, combine tools in a way nobody scripted, and revise the model when evidence contradicts it.
- **Metacognition** is thinking about that thinking. Confidence has to track correctness, because a spoken assurance is cheap. The agent also has to notice a stall, and it has to change behavior at the edge of what it knows.
- The blueprint borrows three patterns. Minsky: many small modules, none of them smart alone. Baars: they share one workspace and broadcast the signal that matters. Kahneman: send familiar work down a fast path and hard work down a slow one.
- Build a shared workspace, then five modules around it: perception frames the task, planning picks or revises a strategy, execution acts, evaluation scores the evidence and the confidence, and attention chooses who goes next. Memory sits beside the workspace as an MCP knowledge graph of problem types, strategies that worked, and links between related problems. Read it before planning. Write the outcome after evaluation.
- The cognitive loop lives inside each agentic iteration. Attention is the inner router. A simple, familiar query can skip the full cycle. Contradiction, a stall, low confidence, or a knowledge gap sends control somewhere else.
- Gate what you show the user on the workspace confidence. A very low score, a falling trend, or a contradiction withholds a polished claim. Detect a stall from overlapping findings or a flat confidence line, record the failed strategy, and pivot so the planner cannot repeat it. When retrieval, memory, and confidence all say the topic is uncovered, say so.
- Curiosity, sticking with a problem, skipping depth on easy questions, and handing back a partial answer with the uncertainty marked all fall out of those signals. Track four numbers: steps taken against the steps the task needed, confidence against real correctness, how fast a failed approach is replaced, and how often the agent is unsure on easy questions or sure on a hallucination.
